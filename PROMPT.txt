**Wedding Invite Generator**

I want an interactive digital wedding invitation — the kind you open on a phone
and scroll through, not a PDF.

**Start from this exact file — it is the invitation I want, working:**
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HirajG/wedding-invite-generator/HEAD/template/invite.html

Fetch that URL and read it. It is one self-contained HTML file. Use it as your
starting point and change only the content: the names, the parents, the dates,
the ceremonies, the venue, the WhatsApp number, the photos and the colours.
Keep its structure, its interactions and its timing exactly as they are — the
envelope unfolding, the tassel pull, the full-screen scenes, the day-to-night
shift, the falling flowers.

There is also a live version you can open to see it in motion:
https://claude.ai/artifact/EmBZBSf7STcRge4BJ5Agvg

If you cannot fetch the file for any reason, tell me in one short line and
build it from the description further down, which covers the same thing.

Please work in three steps and do not skip ahead.

---

## STEP 1 — ask me everything first

Before you write a single line of code, ask me all of this in **one message**,
as a numbered list, then stop and wait for my answers. Keep it friendly and in
plain language — I am not technical, so no jargon. Tell me I can skip any of it
and you will put something tasteful in its place.

1. The couple's first names, and how to write them together
2. Both sets of parents' names, as they should appear
3. Every function: name, day and date, start time, venue, dress code, and
   whether it is daytime or night. There is no fixed number — I might have
   three, I might have nine. I will list them in the order I want them shown.
4. The main wedding date and time
5. The venue name and full address, for directions
6. A WhatsApp number for RSVPs, with country code
7. Any photos I want to upload — one of the couple, and any for the functions
8. A colour direction, or choose one for me

If I upload photos, confirm you can actually see them before you start building.
If I give you a WhatsApp number, repeat it back to me so I can check it.

## STEP 2 — build it

One self-contained artifact, designed for a phone first.

**Scroll order**

1. **A sealed envelope filling the screen.** A hint says to tap. On tap the seal
   cracks with a glint, then four triangular flaps unfold outward on their
   diagonal folds, revealing the invitation. Confetti bursts as it opens.
2. **The names.** A photo in an arched frame, "together with their families",
   both names large, both sets of parents, and a line inviting the reader.
3. **Save the date.** A hanging scroll with gold rods, covered. The reader drags
   a tassel downward to uncover the date. Under it, an "Add to calendar" button
   that opens a prefilled Google Calendar event.
4. **One full-screen scene per function**, in my order. Each fills the whole
   screen with its name, day and date, time, venue, and a dress-code pill with
   a colour swatch.
5. **Our Story** — a few photo moments with short captions.
6. **How to Reach** — a stylised map, the address, a "Get directions" button
   that opens Google Maps *directions* (not just a dropped pin), and a
   "Copy address" button.
7. **RSVP** — a sign-off and a WhatsApp button with a friendly prefilled message.

**The thing that makes it feel expensive**

The background is a **fixed, full-screen stage behind everything**, not a stack
of cards. As I scroll, whichever section is nearest the middle of the screen
takes over that stage, cross-fading over about a second. The whole screen
becomes each function in turn.

**Tie each scene to its time of day.** Daytime functions get warm sunlit sky and
a sun glow; evening ones get deep night sky, moon and stars. The page travels
from morning to night as I scroll. Add light that belongs to each scene — a sun
bloom that pulses, fire flicker at the pheras, chandeliers at the reception,
coloured stage beams at the sangeet.

Over everything, **flowers fall continuously** — marigolds drawn as rings of
petals around a centre, plus rose petals and jasmine, drifting and rotating.

**Details that will otherwise go wrong**

- Put the dark scrim **anchored to the bottom of the screen**, not inside the
  artwork, so text stays readable however the photo crops on a given phone.
- Photos must be **embedded so they travel with the link** — as artifact assets,
  or inlined. Never link to a file on my computer or an outside website; those
  break the moment a guest opens it. Shrink them before embedding so the page
  stays light and the cross-fades don't stutter.
- No photos? Build each scene from gradients, shapes and light, still committing
  to day or night. Never leave a grey box.
- **Crop, don't squash.** Nothing may stretch on a tall phone screen.
- Pair a serif display face with a clean sans, from Google Fonts.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Use however many functions I gave you, in my order. Do not hardcode five.

## STEP 3 — hand it over properly

This matters more than the code, so do not rush it.

When it is built, tell me in plain language:

1. **That the invitation is private right now** and my guests cannot open it yet.
2. **Exactly how to make it shareable** — walk me through it like I have never
   done it before: open the invitation, find the Share control on it, and turn
   on the option that lets anyone with the link view it. Tell me what to look
   for on screen.
3. **How to check it worked** — paste the link into a private/incognito window,
   or send it to one friend first and confirm it opens for them before I send
   it to the whole family.

Do not tell me the link is public until I have confirmed I did that step.

4. **If it did not come out the way I expected, say so plainly and tell me
   why** — and name the actual reason rather than apologising. For example:
   you could not fetch the template so you built it from the description; you
   could not upload image files, so the backgrounds are gradients instead of
   the real artwork; my photos were too large to embed; or I did not give you
   enough detail for a section. Then tell me what would fix it — most often
   that is running this on Claude Desktop or Claude Code with
   github.com/HirajG/wedding-invite-generator, where the artwork can be
   uploaded properly.

Then stay in this chat with me. If I want to change a name, swap a photo,
reorder the functions or fix a time, update the same invitation rather than
making a new one, so the link I already sent out keeps working.
